# 30.10 — Phase 9: Notifications + Operational Risk Analytics

**Status:** engine and notification layer complete and tested; **no new UI is
wired** and the risk feature flag is **deliberately off** (§7)
**Precedence:** `docs/13` and `docs/14` are T0 anchors. `docs/14` names module

all of those are followed.

## 1. What this phase is

Two capabilities:

- **Notifications** — the L5 realtime listener, the row mapper, and the copy
  discipline that keeps a notification from claiming more than the system knows.
- **Analytics** — the metric catalogue, the rollup/live source decision, and the
  risk score, all pure and unit-tested.

**Both notification infrastructure and the analytics data model already existed
from Phases 1–3.** `NOTIFICATION_TYPES` (12 values), `notifications` in
`config/collections.ts`, the `notifications` block in `firestore.rules`,
`Analytics` / `AnalyticsTotals` / `RiskZone` in `types/domain.ts`, nine
`features/analytics/*` components, and `services/dispatch/notify.ts` from Phase 7
with its dedupe key, copy constants and recipient matrix. This phase wires real
data behind them and adds what was missing.

### 1.1 No duplicate models

brief §3 and §4 propose a notification shape (`id`, `userId`, `isRead`, `message`,
`dispatchId`, `metadata`) and eleven type names. The project's existing naming is
different and better-founded:

| brief proposes | project uses | source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id` | `notificationId` | `docs/07 §10.1` |
| `userId` | `recipientUid` — and it is the **query key** (`docs/11 §2.2` L5) | `docs/07 §1` |
| `isRead` | `read` | existing rules + Phase 3 type |
| `message` | `body` | existing |
| `INCIDENT_CREATED` | `incident_created` | `docs/07 §10.2` |

The documented set is used unchanged. `NOTIFICATION_TYPES` was already the 12
`docs/07 §10.2` values, so no enum was added, and a security check asserts the
client mapper **derives** its set from the constant rather than restating it (§5.2).

## 2. A cross-phase bug this phase found

### 2.1 A documentation naming collision, and two consequences

Two documents name the same seven elements differently:

| Set | `docs/07 §12.1` | `docs/11 §2.1` | `docs/14 §2.1` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 values, **no** `resolved` | — | `ACTIVE_STATUSES` | "Active statuses" |
| 7 values, **with** `resolved` | `ACTIVE_STATUSES` | `OPEN_STATUSES` | "Open statuses" |

`types/enums.ts` follows `docs/07 §12.1`, so its `ACTIVE_STATUSES` is the
**seven**-element one. Reading it by `docs/14`'s meaning is wrong in two places,
and Phase 8 made one of them:

1. **Phase 8's live queue showed RESOLVED incidents.** `docs/11 §2.2`'s L1 row
   filters `status in ACTIVE_STATUSES` using *that document's* meaning — the six.
   Phase 8's `QUEUE_STATUS_WINDOW` was built from the seven, so a finished incident
   reappeared in the dispatcher's work queue.
2. **Phase 9's `counts.active` and `counts.resolved` double-counted** every
   resolved incident. A test asserting the buckets are disjoint failed, which is how
   the collision was found rather than shipped.

**Fixed in both places.** `lib/collections/enums.ts` now carries
`LIVE_ACTIVE_STATUSES` (6) with the collision documented in full, plus
`OPEN_STATUSES` (7) and `ANALYTICS_ACTIVE_STATUSES` (6). Phase 3's array is
**unchanged** — a test pins its length at 7 for a documented reason ("a
resolved-but-not-closed incident still has an open audit obligation"), and that
reason is about the *incident* being open, not about it being in a work queue.

### 2.2 `AnalyticsTotals` could not express "not enough data"

`docs/14 §2.1`: "A metric whose inputs are absent is **`null`, never `0`. `0`
asserts 'we measured zero'; `null` asserts 'we could not measure this'." brief §30
says the same.

Phase 3 typed every total as `number`, which made the rule **unimplementable** — a
duration computed from zero qualifying records had to be *some* number, and
`formatDuration(0)` reads on a dispatcher's screen as *"resolved instantly"*, which
is the opposite of the truth and would suggest they could stop dispatching.

Fixed: `MaybeNumber = number | null` widens the six durations and rates while the
**counts stay `number`**, because a count of zero is a real measurement of a
window. `kpi-grid.tsx` renders `NO_DATA = 'Not enough data'`, and
`slaVariant(null)` is `'default'` — **not** `'breached'`, because a red SLA tile is
a thing a dispatcher acts on and it must not appear for missing data.

## 3. `lib/analytics/` — the engine

### 3.1 `decideSource` — `docs/14 §3.2`, FR-116

```ts
rollup when the period ENDS more than 48 h ago, else live
```

- **Keys on the END, not the start.** A 90-day view ending today is LIVE: the
  decision depends on the *freshest* data in the window, and a start-keyed rule
  would show data up to 48 h stale while the two most recent days were available.
- **48 h, not 24 h.** "It gives one full day of slack. If yesterday's cron fails at
  03:00, the `to = yesterday` view still works from rollups."

`docs/14` names the file `lib/analytics/decideSource.ts`; the module is
`decide-source` to match this project's kebab-case convention.

### 3.2 `time.ts` — local day boundaries, and why `Date` cannot do this

`new Date('2026-09-26')` parses as **UTC** midnight, which in Asia/Kolkata is
05:30 into the day. An "all of 26 September" bucket would start five and a half
hours in, and the last incident of the previous evening would be counted in the
wrong bucket. That is not a rounding error — it is the difference between a
dispatcher seeing "3 incidents today" and seeing three of which one happened at
23:30 yesterday.

The offset is resolved through `Intl.DateTimeFormat` for the **specific instant**,
so a zone with DST gets a correct boundary on each day, and
`endOfLocalDayMs` is computed as *the next local day's start minus 1 ms* rather
than `start + 86 400 000` — a DST day is 23 or 25 hours long.

`daysInRange` is **bounded at 366** and stops rather than spinning on a reversed or
malformed range.

**The timezone is a required parameter with no default.** `docs/21` records
`APP_TIMEZONE` as a *server* variable, so `time.ts` does not read it — a default
would be a lie with a convenient signature, because a caller who forgot the zone
would get UTC boundaries and a plausible-looking chart with the last few hours of
each day in the wrong bucket.

### 3.3 `metrics.ts` — the `docs/14 §2` catalogue

| Group | Metrics |
| --- | --- |
| Volume (§2.2) | `total`, `active`, per-urgency + `unknown`, per-terminal-status, `reports`, `linkedReports` |
| Time (§2.3) | `meanTimeToVerifySec`, `meanTimeToDispatchSec`, `meanTimeToResolveSec` |
| Rates (§2.4) | `slaCompliancePct`, `duplicateRatePct`, `aiFallbackRatePct`, `meanAiConfidence`, `reportsPerIncident` |
| Location (§2.5) | `topLocations` (geohash-6 cell counts), `densityPerCell` |
| Human (§2.6) | `avgPeopleAffected` **with `sampleSize`** |

Decisions worth stating:

- **`meanTimeToDispatchSec` and `meanTimeToResolveSec` use `verifiedAt ?? createdAt`.**
  `docs/14 §2.3`: "FR-019 allows a citizen to cancel before verification, and the
  fallback triage path (FR-029)…" — measuring from `verifiedAt` alone would drop
  every unverified incident, which is exactly the set most likely to have been
  fast.
- **A negative duration is `null`,** not a negative number. Host clock skew and a
  Firestore timestamp microseconds ahead of the caller's clock both produce one,
  and a negative response time in an ops report reads as data.
- **`merged` is counted separately and never folded into `resolved`** — folding it
  in would inflate the resolution count and make the resolution analytics wrong.
- **An incident with no `urgency` is counted in an `unknown` band,** not dropped.
  brief §16 asks for an Unknown band, and a distribution that omits its largest
  category is not a distribution. `URGENCIES` has four values and no `unknown`
  member, so the band is a *count of nulls*, not a fifth enum value.
- **`slaCompliancePct` does not count an in-flight incident as compliant** — that
  would inflate the rate by whatever is in the queue, and improve as the backlog
  grew.
- **`topLocations` groups on `geoCells[0]`, the incident's own centre cell.** An
  incident's `geoCells` is a 9-cell block; grouping on the whole block would
  double-count every incident nine times. Incidents with **no position are counted
  and reported** as `withoutPosition`, not dropped.
- **`avgPeopleAffected` returns its `sampleSize` too.** FR-023 makes the field
  null-heavy by design, and a mean over three records is a very different claim
  from a mean over three hundred.

### 3.4 `risk-score.ts` — `docs/14 §6.3` transcribed

| Component | Formula | Weight |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| `density` | `min(count / (windowDays × 4), 1)` | 0.50 |
| `severity` | `(critical×1.0 + high×0.5 + medium×0.2) / max(count, 1)` | 0.35 |
| `recency` | `exp(-ln2 × days / 14)` | 0.15 |

`WEIGHT_RECENCY` is a **constant, not a third parameter** — `docs/14 §6.3` says it
is "fixed by the formula", and making it configurable would let the three weights
stop summing to 1 and produce a score above 100.

The score is also returned **with its three components**, because FR-115 requires
the parameters to be stored for reproducibility and a score with no decomposition
is a number nobody can challenge.

## 4. The honesty requirement — brief §2, §20; `docs/14 §6`, §6.8

### 4.1 `RiskZoneInput` is the enforcement

`docs/14 §6`: "This is a heuristic that summarises past incidents. It is not a
prediction, and it does not forecast."

`RiskZoneInput` has three counts and two durations. There is no `population`, no
`forecast`, no `weather`, no `model` — **and no field that could carry one without
a type change.** That is the enforcement: a developer cannot add a predictor
without changing the type, and a security check asserts the field set and that no
field is *named* predictively.

### 4.2 The statement is verbatim, and the flag is off

`RISK_HONESTY_STATEMENT` is copied from `docs/14 §6.8` character for character,
as a **constant** so a security check can assert the exact text and a reworded
paragraph is a visible diff in one place rather than a softened sentence in a
component. It opens "**This is a summary of past incidents, not a forecast.**" and
names the three limits: population, weather, and reporting propensity.

`RISK_ZONES_ENABLED = false`, because `docs/14 §6.1` says **default `false` (P1)**
and this phase does not change it. The computation is implemented and tested; the
flag gates whether it is *served*. A density heatmap enabled by default is the most
over-claimable surface in the product.

`FORBIDDEN_RISK_CLAIMS` holds the sentences brief §20 names ("This area will be
dangerous", "AI predicts an accident here", "Guaranteed high-risk zone") plus
three more, and a check scans the analytics components for them **after stripping
comments** — because a comment is where a prohibition gets *described*.

## 5. Notifications

### 5.1 L5 is scoped, structurally

`docs/11 §11.3` SEC-4: "L5's query is `where('recipientUid','==',uid)` and the hook
exposes **no parameter** to widen it."

`useRealtimeNotifications` declares **exactly one option, `seed`.** The check
asserts the *set* of declared properties, because the first version asserted a
signature **prefix** and a mutation appending `recipientUid?:` after `seed?` did
not break it — a prefix test cannot detect an addition.

A notification is **targeted correspondence about one user's emergency** ("an
incident was assigned to you", "your report was resolved"). A query that could be
widened to another recipient would be a read of somebody else's operational mail.

`firestore.rules` already scopes `notifications` reads to `recipientUid ==
auth.uid`, refuses `create`/`delete`, and restricts the client's only write to
`read` / `readAt` / `updatedAt` (SEC-5). Check 69 asserts all three, because the
rule is the boundary and the query is a convenience.

### 5.2 The unread count is derived, and the type set is derived

The unread count is `items.filter(unread).length` over the listener's own
50-document window, which `docs/11 §11.2` permits ("derived from the same 50
-document snapshot"). It is therefore the count **of unread among the 50 most
recent**, and `unreadLabel` caps at `99+` so a three-digit number cannot push the
top-bar layout on a narrow screen.

The type set is `new Set<string>(NOTIFICATION_TYPES)` — **derived, not restated.**
The first draft listed the twelve by hand; the same mistake in the incident row
mapper silently dropped a real `duplicateStatus`, and here an unrecognised type
degrades to a neutral badge, so a real "incident assigned" would render as generic.

### 5.3 Phase 7's copy discipline carries forward

`NOTIFICATION_COPY` and `FORBIDDEN_NOTIFICATION_CLAIMS` from Phase 7 are unchanged
and still asserted. The subtler rule still holds: **acceptance is not reported as
departure** — only the second would support "on the way".

### 6.1 Three check bugs the mutation testing found

All three had the same shape — a check that could not see the thing it was
watching, and so passed for the wrong reason:

- **#60** extracted field names with `readonly (\w+):`, which does not match
  `readonly forecastProbability?:`. A **predictive field was invisible to the check
  that exists to prevent one.** Rewritten to allow the `?` and to assert both
  "exactly the six" and "no predictively-named field".
- **#67** asserted the hook's signature *prefix*. Appending an option did not break
  it. Rewritten to assert the **set** of declared properties.
- **#62**'s first mutation appended a forbidden phrase to the first occurrence of
  `RiskSection`, which is inside that symbol's **JSDoc block** — and the check
  strips comments before scanning. The check was right; the mutation was invalid.
  Three earlier "misses" in this project had the same cause, which is now annotated
  in the check.

| **No `analyticsDaily` writer** | The rollup *path* is chosen by `decideSource` and the index is declared; nothing computes or writes a daily rollup, and no cron is scheduled. The rollup branch of the decision therefore has no data behind it yet. |

| **No listener has ever executed** | No deployed index, no browser. The queries and metrics are unit-tested for shape; nothing has run. |

### 7.1 Why the UI was not wired

The analytics components already exist and already look correct; what they lack is
a data source. Building `/api/analytics` plus a rollup writer is a substantial
sub-project on its own, and shipping the components against `MOCK_ANALYTICS` with
new *types* would have made the mock fail to compile in a way that either forces a
cast or forces the `MaybeNumber` widening back out — undoing the §2.2 fix. So the
type and the rendering are honest, and the data path is the honest gap.

### 8.1 Three things Phase 10 must not undo

1. **`MaybeNumber` in `AnalyticsTotals`.** Narrowing it back to `number` would make
   "not enough data" unrepresentable, and the zero would render as a measured value.
2. **`LIVE_ACTIVE_STATUSES` (6) vs `OPEN_STATUSES` (7).** Re-reading
   `types/enums.ts`'s `ACTIVE_STATUSES` by `docs/14`'s meaning puts resolved
   incidents back in the queue and double-counts them in the analytics.
3. **The honesty statement verbatim.** It is a product promise, and paraphrasing it
   is the single change that would turn a descriptive score into a forecast in the
   reader's mind.

Phase 8 §7). Every listener in this phase and the last one leaks across a sign-out
until that is fixed. It is a one-line call in `hooks/useAuth.ts` and it is the
highest-value remaining item in the codebase.
